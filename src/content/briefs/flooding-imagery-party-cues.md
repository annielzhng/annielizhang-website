## What we did

We tested how the images and political signals in news stories shape views of flooding and climate change. Some stories showed dramatic photos of floods, while others showed maps and graphs. Some stories also mentioned where Republicans and Democrats stood on the issue.

## What we found

- Dramatic flood photos made people feel more threatened by flooding than maps and graphs did. Neither kind of image changed views of climate change overall or narrowed the gap between liberals and conservatives.
- Mentioning party positions made people feel less threatened by flooding.
- Feeling more threatened increased people’s belief that we can act together on climate change, which in turn predicted their intentions to take political action.

## Why it matters

For polarized audiences, focusing on concrete threats such as flooding or air pollution may work better than talking about climate change in general. Communicators should also watch how partisan cues can dull a sense of danger.
