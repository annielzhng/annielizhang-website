## What we did

We showed more than 1,500 people Twitter profiles posting about climate change. Some belonged to scientists and some to influencers, and the posts were either personal or professional. We measured how authentic and knowledgeable each poster seemed, how connected people felt to them (what researchers call parasocial interaction), and whether they wanted to learn more or act, such as by donating to a climate organization.

## What we found

- Scientists were rated as more authentic and more qualified than influencers.
- Profiles that shared professional details were rated as more qualified than profiles that shared personal ones, whether the poster was a scientist or an influencer.
- The more connected people felt to a poster, the more likely they were to seek information and take positive action. Scientists were better at building that connection.
- People high in anti-intellectualism trusted both sources less, but influencers still moved them to act more than scientists did.

## Why it matters

Knowing that some audiences distrust experts helps communicators tailor their messages, and influencers may be valuable partners for reaching those audiences. For any messenger, authenticity builds trust.
