## What we did

We showed 484 people tweets about mRNA COVID-19 vaccines from Dr. Finley Jones, a fictional scientist we created for the study. The tweets used no humor, light-hearted humor, or biting satire. We measured whether people would engage with the tweet and how they felt about the vaccines.

## What we found

- Both humorous and satirical tweets surprised people, made them less likely to engage, and increased their skepticism about the vaccines.
- People high in a trait we call superiority were less surprised by the humor and more receptive to it.

## Why it matters

Humor can make science more engaging, but audiences still expect scientists to stay serious on serious topics. Scientists should weigh those expectations before joking about high-stakes issues.
