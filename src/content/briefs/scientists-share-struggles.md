## What we did

In early 2024, scientists on Twitter started posting about their academic rejections instead of their wins. We wanted to know how that kind of honesty lands with the public. We showed 1,851 people posts from scientists. Some celebrated successes. Others described experiments that went wrong and papers that were turned down. Then we measured how people saw the scientists and how interested they became in the science.

## What we found

- Scientists who shared failures were rated as more trustworthy, more open, and more kind-hearted than scientists who shared only accomplishments.
- People who saw the scientists as genuine and relatable were more likely to support science and to look for more information about it.

## Why it matters

Science rarely moves in a straight line, but public posts usually make it look that way. Sharing setbacks shows the trial and error behind discovery, which makes scientists more human and their work more approachable.
